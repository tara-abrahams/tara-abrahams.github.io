About

Background & biography

Tara R. Abrahams is a criminologist, qualitative researcher, and strategic communicator whose work focuses on how people make decisions in high-stakes, constrained environments. She is currently pursuing her PhD in Criminology and Criminal Justice at Georgia State University, where her research examines offender decision-making and the self-imposed limits that shape criminal behavior.

Before graduate school, Tara served for about ten years in the U.S. Air Force Reserve as a public affairs specialist, including several years on active-duty orders supporting higher headquarters and command-level communication teams. Originally trained as a military photojournalist, she later expanded into video and broader multimedia public affairs — producing written stories, photography, and video; managing social media and web content; and advising on communication strategy. Assigned to a wing-level public affairs office, she translated complex and sensitive topics into clear public communication for local units and command-level audiences alike. She reached the rank of Technical Sergeant (E-6) and has since fully separated from military service, with no ongoing service obligations.

Tara's current work bridges rigorous qualitative research and applied communication. She has experience conducting in-depth interviews, analyzing narrative data, and presenting findings in accessible formats for non-academic stakeholders. Across her academic and professional roles, she has developed a portfolio of writing and visual storytelling that reflects a consistent focus on behavior, context, and the real-world implications of policy and practice. That combination of behavioral research and applied communication translates naturally to industry roles that blend research and communication work — including Trust & Safety research, UX or behavioral research, and research-informed communications more broadly.

Dissertation

Drawing the Line: Self-Imposed Limits in Offending and Offender Decision-Making

This research examines how active offenders construct, maintain, and articulate personal limits on their criminal behavior. Drawing on qualitative interviews and narrative analysis, the project explores the cognitive and social processes that govern decision-making within criminal careers.

Methods

  • In-depth qualitative interviewing
  • Narrative and thematic analysis
  • Research with active and formerly incarcerated populations
  • IRB-compliant human-subjects research
  • Qualitative data coding and analysis

About

Background & biography

Tara R. Abrahams is pursuing a PhD in Criminology and Criminal Justice at Georgia State University. Her dissertation, Drawing the Line: Offender Limits and Harm Control Across Offline, Online, and Platform Contexts, examines how people draw, negotiate, and respond to boundaries around harmful or rule-breaking behavior across offline, online, and platform contexts. This research connects directly to Trust & Safety work: it speaks to how people interpret and evade rules, how harmful behavior develops and is constrained, how people respond to safety interventions, and the practical consequences of platform governance.

Before graduate school, Tara served for about ten years in the U.S. Air Force Reserve as a public affairs specialist and has since fully separated from military service, with no ongoing obligations. She was initially trained as a military photojournalist and later expanded into writing, photography, video, social media, and web content. Assigned to a wing-level public affairs office, she also spent several years on active-duty orders supporting higher headquarters and command-level communication teams, translating complex or sensitive topics into clear, accurate communication for broad audiences.

Tara's applied communication and operations experience also extends into scholarly publishing and customer-facing work. She previously served as Managing Editor of Production for Criminology, a Wiley journal, where she coordinated a 28-step workflow from manuscript acceptance through publication and supported the timely, accurate dissemination of peer-reviewed research. She has also worked as a customer support and fraud specialist, assisting customers and helping address fraudulent account activity.

Together, this background — qualitative research on rule-breaking and harm, applied communication, and operational experience translating complex material for broad audiences — supports industry work in Trust & Safety research, research-informed communications, and related behavioral-research roles.

Academic Work

Research

Tara is an active PhD researcher whose work is organized around three connected pillars: offender limits and decision-making, harmful and abusive behavior, and harm control, Trust & Safety, and online governance.

Dissertation

Drawing the Line: Offender Limits and Harm Control Across Offline, Online, and Platform Contexts

This dissertation examines offender decision-making and the self-imposed limits some people maintain even while engaging in harmful or rule-breaking behavior. The project looks at harm control across offline, online, and platform contexts — how people interpret rules, decide whether and how to comply with or evade them, and respond to enforcement and safety interventions. This work speaks directly to Trust & Safety, online harms, and platform governance, where similar questions about rule-breaking, compliance, and the consequences of intervention are central.

Featured Completed Research

Comparing the Motivation of Auto Thieves and Carjackers
Master’s Thesis, 2022

This qualitative thesis compares the motivations and decision-making of people who committed auto theft and carjacking. It examines how offenders weighed financial gain, transportation needs, pleasure, violence, interpersonal risk, and anticipated consequences when selecting an offense type and carrying it out.

Drawing on qualitative analysis of offender accounts, this project examined motivation, perceived risk, harm, and self-imposed limits — laying groundwork for Tara’s current research on offender decision-making, harmful behavior, rule-breaking, and harm control.

Related conference presentation: “The time cultures of auto thieves and carjackers” (ASC 2024 poster) — see Presentations below.
